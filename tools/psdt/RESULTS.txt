    chroma kept   chroma retained against the scene colour scaled to the same output
                  luminance. Unflattering by construction - the reference is often a
                  colour no display can show - so it is only comparable, not a target.
    boundary use  output chroma as a fraction of the most the display can show at that
                  output luminance and hue. This is the one that says whether the
                  transform is doing as well as physically possible. 1.0 is the wall.
    max hue step  largest hue change between adjacent samples of a 1/42-stop sweep,
                  counted only while the output still has a tenth of the display's chroma
                  capacity - hue is undefined at white and every operator scores badly on
                  a raw sweep. Large = a visible discontinuity on the path to white.
    midtone contr display stops per scene stop across the anchor. 1.0 is flat.
    Y inversions  times a brighter scene value produced a darker display value.
                  Anything but 0 breaks the most basic perceptual invariant there is.
