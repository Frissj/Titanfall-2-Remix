GT7SPACE  what GT7's Rec.2020 input assumption costs
----------------------------------------------------
  gt7.slangh says "input is assumed to be linear Rec.2020 frame-buffer RGB",
  and feeds the framebuffer straight into a Rec.2020 -> LMS matrix. This
  renderer's framebuffer is linear Rec.709 - observably, since the histogram,
  the auto exposure and the colour grading all weight it with Rec.709 luma and
  the apply shader ends with the sRGB OETF and no primary conversion.

  The forward and inverse inside GT7 use the same (wrong) matrices, so an
  untouched colour round-trips exactly and the error is invisible on a grey
  ramp. It enters only where the ICtCp values are modified - the chroma fade

COHERENCE  what moved across when Auto Exposure Plus stopped running
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  v0.3 stops running Plus while PSDT owns local adaptation, on the grounds that
  two multi-scale reprojected pyramids over one buffer is one too many. That is
  only defensible if what Plus was contributing came with it, so this measures
  the two things it did that PSDT's pooling did not.

  1. Edge-aware downsample. A coarse texel whose four children straddle a
     boundary. The plain weighted mean lands between two populations and
     describes neither; the robust reduction reports the one most of them are
     in. "wanted" is that population, or the mean where there is no majority.

    0.0040 stops. A monotone gradient has no majority to find, so the
    robust answer is the plain one - correct, rather than a failure to fire.
    An even split has no majority either, and a one-step reweight cannot
    invent one: both halves sit the same distance from the mean and are
    weighted identically, so that texel still reports a value neither half is
    at. What saves it is that the finer level it was reduced from still holds
    both, is still pooled, and is where a feature that small belongs.

CONFIDENCE  telling a firefly from a muzzle flash
-------------------------------------------------
  A path traced frame can put a hundredfold luminance spike in one block for one
  frame. Nothing in a framebuffer separates that from a real flash; where it was
  a frame ago does. Disagreement widens the history window the block has to
  escape, so appearing costs something and persisting does not.

  What a one-frame spike is worth. Glare responds to log2(1 + energy) rather
  than to energy, so the halo column is the one that says what is visible.

                            peak energy  halo (log2)   of v0.2
    v0.2 (no test)                126.5         6.99    100.0%
    v0.3                           14.1         3.91     55.9%

  What it costs a real source. The same block, with the source still there.

                                50%      90%      99%    halo at 0.1 s
    v0.2 (no test)            0.083    0.367    0.783 s           7.84
    v0.3                      0.200    0.500    0.917 s           6.41

  And the check that only one direction was slowed. A source that has been
  established for half a second, switched off: time to fall below 1% of it.

                             release
    v0.2 (no test)            0.017s
    v0.3                      0.017s

    The release is unchanged, and has to be: the clamp's upper bound is what
    handles a falling measurement and nothing here touches it. A light going
    out is not a thing to be sceptical about.

TEMPORAL  step response of two filters in series
------------------------------------------------
  PSDT has two temporal filters: the adaptation field accumulates with motion
  reprojection, and the anchor accumulates again on top of it. That is good for
  stability and it is also how an image ends up still showing yesterday's
  exposure. v0.1 asserted this was fine; this measures it.

  Within the cut detector: a 2-stop change, which is a real lighting change and
  not a scene change. This is the transform's actual time constant.
    dark -> bright, +2 stops
      anchor 10/50/90%   0.117 / 0.400 / 1.000 s   overshoot +0.0000 st   residual -0.0000 st   cuts fired 0
    bright -> dark, -2 stops
      anchor 10/50/90%   0.200 / 0.750 / 2.100 s   overshoot +0.0000 st   residual +0.0019 st   cuts fired 0
    dark -> bright, +2 stops, mid fast turn
      anchor 10/50/90%   0.067 / 0.233 / 0.550 s   overshoot +0.0000 st   residual -0.0000 st   cuts fired 0

  A 6-stop change spread over half a second - walking through a doorway. Each
  frame's step is small, so the cut detector correctly stays out of it.
    doorway, +6 stops over 0.5 s
      anchor 10/50/90%   0.317 / 0.683 / 1.283 s   overshoot +0.0000 st   residual -0.0000 st   cuts fired 0
    doorway, -6 stops over 0.5 s
      anchor 10/50/90%   0.433 / 1.033 / 2.383 s   overshoot +0.0000 st   residual +0.0078 st   cuts fired 0

  And 6 stops in a single frame, which is not a lighting change - nothing in a
  physical scene does that. Both detectors should fire and the state should
  re-anchor rather than easing through several seconds of wash-out.
    instant +6 stops (luminance cut)
      anchor 10/50/90%   0.000 / 0.000 / 0.017 s   overshoot +0.0000 st   residual +0.0000 st   cuts fired 1
    instant +6 stops (camera reports a cut)
      anchor 10/50/90%   0.000 / 0.000 / 0.017 s   overshoot +0.0000 st   residual +0.0000 st   cuts fired 1

    Up is faster than down by design - light adaptation is fast, dark adaptation
    is slow - and that asymmetry is what makes an interior read as dark when you
    step into it. A fast turn triples the rate, so sweeping your view does not
    drag seconds of wash-out behind it. A cut re-anchors outright.

    Note the field's own +-3 stop history clamp, which is doing real work here:
    it bounds how far behind the measurement the field can ever be, so even the
    un-cut path cannot lag more than three stops however slow the anchor is.

    chroma kept   chroma retained against the scene colour scaled to the same output
                  luminance. Unflattering by construction - the reference is often a
                  colour no display can show - so it is only comparable, not a target.
    boundary use  output chroma as a fraction of the most the display can show at that
                  output luminance and hue. This is the one that says whether the
                  transform is doing as well as physically possible. 1.0 is the wall.
    max hue step  largest hue change between adjacent samples of a 1/42-stop sweep,
                  counted only while the output still has a tenth of the display's chroma
                  capacity - hue is undefined at white and every operator scores badly on
                  a raw sweep. Large = a visible discontinuity on the path to white.
    midtone contr display stops per scene stop across the anchor. 1.0 is flat.
    hilite bnd use  boundary utilisation restricted to 1-4 stops past the shoulder, which
                  is where a bright emissive actually sits. Raw chroma is the wrong measure
                  up there: at 256x middle grey the correct answer really is white, so raw
                  chroma just rewards whichever operator refuses to reach peak luminance.
    Y inversions  times a brighter scene value produced a darker display value.
                  Anything but 0 breaks the most basic perceptual invariant there is.

    SL  luminance fidelity: fraction of the grid that did not clip. Zero if the
        operator ever inverted brightness ordering, because that is disqualifying
        rather than merely costly.
    SC  colour-volume fidelity: mean boundary utilisation, capped at 1.
    SH  hue trajectory smoothness: exp(-maxstep / 1 degree).
    SD  detail: midtone slope mapped from 0.8 (flat) to 1.3.
    ST  temporal stability: exp(-worst frame-to-frame anchor step / 0.1 stops).
        A pure function of one pixel scores 1 by construction - it has no state to
        be unstable, which is also why it cannot adapt to anything.

    than a missing one, because its numbers would look just as authoritative.
